Boosting Automatic Crop Diseases Classification using Sentinel Satellite Data and Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)

KEYWORDS: SSL, ResNet, MoCo, Classification, Crop Disease
ABSTRACT:
Accurate crop disease classification is crucial for precision agriculture, yet acquiring labeled satellite imagery remains expensive and time-consuming. This study investigates self-supervised learning (SSL) approaches to address the challenge of limited labelled data in remote sensing. We evaluate the MoCo v2 contrastive learning framework with a ResNet50 encoder and its associated projection head to perform contrastive pretraining on unlabeled data. The encoder was pretrained twice: first on a subset of 32,567 patches of the unlabeled data, and again on the entire unlabeled dataset consisting of 166,775 patches. Following pretraining, the projection head was replaced with a linear classifier for supervised downstream classification. Using 900 labeled crop disease images from the ICPR 2026 competition, we trained and evaluated classification performance across four disease categories (Aphid, Blast, RPH, and Rust). Our results demonstrate that SSL pretraining on the subset yielded a macro F1-score of 68.7% outperforming both the full dataset pretraining (64.9% Macro F1-score) and the ResNet50 ImageNet baseline (58.7% Macro F1-score). These findings indicate that task-specific SSL pretraining might be critical for optimal performance in label-scarce scenarios. We hypothesize that the performance gap may stem from the fact that, as MoCo v2 lacks temporal capability, each timestamp in the full dataset was treated as an independent sample. This introduced greater spectral and phenological variability during pretraining, potentially pushing the model toward overly general representations less suited for the task.
