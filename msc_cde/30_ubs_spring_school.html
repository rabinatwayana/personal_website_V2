AI4EO 2026 Spring School

  • Location : Université Bretagne Sud (UBS), Vannes, France
  • Date : April 8–10, 2026

Overview

The AI4EO Spring School 2026 was organised as an intensive three-day academic and hands-on training programme at Université Bretagne Sud (UBS), Vannes, France, from 8–10 April 2026. The programme brought together postgraduate students, early-career researchers, Earth Observation (EO) enthusiasts, and professionals with an interest in exploring the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into Earth Observation applications.

The event was jointly organised by the OBELIX group, the EMJM Copernicus Master in Digital Earth, Cluster SequoIA, and ESA Φ-lab. Its main objective was to strengthen participants' theoretical understanding and practical skills in applying AI techniques to Earth Observation. Particular attention was given to emerging research areas, including EO foundation models, responsible AI, Machine Learning Operations (MLOps), and generative AI models. In addition to expert lectures, participants engaged in practical coding exercises and a data-driven project challenge supported by ESA Φ-lab, providing opportunities to apply the concepts covered during the programme to real-world problems.

The spring school also offered the opportunity to learn from internationally recognised researchers and specialists working in artificial intelligence and remote sensing. The invited speakers included Prof. Pedram Ghamisi, Nicolas Audebert, Iris de Gelis, Adam Stewart, Stéphane May, Pierre Adorni, Weikang Yu, and Sébastien Lefèvre. Through their lectures and interactive workshops, they shared their expertise on recent scientific developments, advanced technical approaches, and practical applications of AI in remote sensing and Earth Observation.

The first day centred on foundation models and advanced remote sensing applications. Iris de Gelis opened the programme with a session on deep learning for change detection in 3D point clouds, highlighting how AI techniques can identify temporal changes and extract features from LiDAR and other geospatial data. Later, Stéphane May and Pierre Adorni introduced Foundation Models for Earth Observation, discussing their potential to improve model generalisation across satellite datasets and EO tasks. Practical sessions allowed participants to explore their implementation, benefits, and limitations in real-world EO workflows.

The second day focused on Responsible AI and the operational deployment of machine learning models. Prof. Pedram Ghamisi discussed ethical AI principles, including transparency, fairness, robustness, and reproducibility, highlighting their importance in reliable EO applications. Weikang Yu complemented the lecture with a practical session on explainable AI, model evaluation, and bias reduction. Additionally, Adam Stewart introduced TorchGeo and MLOps, demonstrating scalable and reproducible workflows for processing geospatial data and deploying AI models in real-world Earth Observation applications.

The third day explored generative AI applications in Earth Observation, with Nicolas Audebert leading lectures and coding sessions on image generation, data augmentation, super-resolution, and addressing gaps in remote sensing datasets. A key highlight was the ESA Φ-lab project challenge, where participants worked in teams to apply the techniques learned during the school to practical EO problems, enhancing their collaboration and problem-solving skills. The programme also provided valuable networking opportunities through social activities, encouraging knowledge exchange and potential research collaborations among participants from diverse academic and professional backgrounds.

Alongside the academic sessions, the spring school organised social activities to encourage interaction and networking among participants and instructors. One of the highlights was a boat trip along the coast of Vannes on the final day. This informal gathering provided a relaxed setting for participants to connect, exchange experiences, and strengthen relationships with fellow attendees and faculty members.
