AI4EO Symposium 2025

  • Event Detail : https://ai4eo2025.irisa.fr
  • Duration : Septemper 11-12, 2025
  • Location : Rennes, Brittany, France

Overview

The AI4EO Symposium, launched in 2022 under the International AI Future Lab AI4EO, is a leading forum at the intersection of artificial intelligence and Earth observation. Following three successful editions in Munich, the 2025 edition will take place in Rennes, France (September 11–12). The symposium brings together researchers, students, and industry experts for keynote talks, oral and poster sessions, panel discussions, and networking, fostering collaboration and innovation in AI4EO.

Day 1 (11 September 2025)

The first day offered a well-structured program combining keynote lectures, research oral presentations, poster presentation and discussions that encouraged knowledge exchange between academia and industry. Among the highlights were two keynote addresses that set the tone for the symposium, providing valuable insights into current challenges and future directions in the field of AI for Earth Observation:
  • Keynote 1: Responsible AI for Earth Observation by Caroline Gevaert.

    Caroline Gevaert, Associate Professor at the University of Twente and part-time advisor to the World Bank, focuses on applying geospatial AI (GeoAI) for sustainable development. In her talk, she outlined the core aspects of Explainable AI—privacy, accountability, safety and security, fairness and non-discrimination, human oversight, transferability, and interpretability. Her central message emphasized that AI models should remain transparent and understandable to end users.

  • Keynote 2: Destination Earth: building a digital replica of the Earth system by Bertrand Le Saux

    Bertrand Le Saux is policy officer for Green Deal (Destination Earth) and AI applications in DG CNECT at the European Commission. In his talk, he focussed on importance of DTs to adapt and mitigate climate change impacts.

Day 2 (12 September 2025)
The second day also offered similar program that includes keynote lectures, research oral presentations, poster presentation and discussions, and panel discussion in addition. The two interesting keynotes presentation are as follows:
  • Keynote 1: Machine learning for large-scale ecosystem monitoring by Christian Igel

    Christian Igel is a professor at DIKU, the Department of Computer Science at the University of Copenhagen, and serves as the director of the SCIENCE AI Centre. In his talk, he presented a variety of projects, ranging from monitoring tree ecosystems and estimating biomass from LiDAR point clouds to addressing seamlines in satellite mosaics, assessing the risk of vector-borne diseases, and mapping ancient dry-stone monuments in the Sahara. These initiatives ultimately contribute to advancing the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

  • Keynote 2: Large Foundation models and their adaptation to remote sensing by Maria Vakalopoulou

    Maria Vakalopoulou has been an Assistant Professor since 2019 and leads the βiomathematics group at the MICS laboratory, CentraleSupélec, University Paris-Saclay in Paris, France. In her presentations, she discussed various large deep learning models, their applications, experimental results on Earth Observation (EO) data, and model adaptation strategies. The findings demonstrate that vision and vision-language models outperform traditionally trained models in conformal prediction, with DINO and CLIP emerging as the leading architectures among visual transformers and CNNs. APS achieves the best balance of coverage and robustness against distributional shifts, while RAPS provides an effective alternative for controlling conformal set size. Furthermore, few-shot adaptation of vision-language models proves more accurate than zero-shot approaches, highlighting their potential to enhance predictive performance.
